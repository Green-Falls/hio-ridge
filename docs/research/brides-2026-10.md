# What couples weigh when they choose a venue (research, October 19, 2026)

Read before the round 5 copy and UX pass. Sources are mixed: two large surveys, the rest trade blogs. Weight accordingly.

## The surveys
- **WeddingPro / The Knot Venue Selection Study** (781 US couples) and the 2022 Real Weddings Study (11,646): the most important factors are price, having ceremony and reception in one place, and availability. Couples inquire with five venues and tour four. 79% had no must-have date when they started looking. Autumn is the favourite season (about 45%). Nearly 80% start researching venues within two months of the engagement. Source: pros.weddingpro.com/report/what-couples-really-want-from-their-wedding-venues.
- **Zola First Look Report 2026** (about 11,500 couples marrying in 2026): outdoor garden venues are the first choice for the second year, rustic farms and barns next. Couples choose a venue partly for how it will photograph, and rule venues out that do not meet their visual goals. 87% made at least one planning choice from social media. Average guest count 145; average cost $36,000; 48% raised their budget to get something they saw online. Source: zola.com/expert-advice/the-first-look-report-2026.
- **The Knot Real Weddings Study 2026** (10,000+ couples married in 2025): 117 guests, $34,000 average; 52% say responsiveness is key to trusting a vendor; 36% used AI in planning; 37% contacted more vendors than planned to fit a budget. Source: theknotww.com press release, February 18, 2026.

## The tour checklists (The Knot, Venue Crew, WedSafe, Mindy Weiss)
Couples arrive with the same list: the date and how soon they must commit; off-peak discounts; capacity and seating configuration; the access window and end time; private getting-ready rooms; accessibility; whether children are allowed; vendor restrictions; parking and lodging; the rain plan; who they will deal with on the day.

## Sundays and alcohol-free, the two things that make this venue different
- Sunday: vendors are more available and often cheaper; guests who travel get a longer weekend; the caveat is hair and makeup availability and Monday work. Sources: The Knot "Sunday weddings", Planterra, Green Acres.
- Dry: about 6% of 2025 couples planned a fully alcohol-free wedding (Zola, via the Wall Street Journal); reasons given are cost, children and family, driving home safely, faith, and sober-curious guests. The pushback is real (a CNN poll found only 8% happy with the idea), so the site states it as a fact and a family-day benefit, never as a selling point on its own.
