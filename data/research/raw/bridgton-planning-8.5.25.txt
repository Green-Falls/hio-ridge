Item #6         Old Business (Continued)

          6a.   Bridgton Food Pantry
                Main St
                Map 25 Lot 4
                        Public Hearing

Reed Holden, representing the Board of the Bridgton Food Pantry stated that the property was
purchased in 2023 and since then, they have been working on fundraising and this application. He also
stated that there has been over $26000 raised above the anticipated cost of the building.

Tom Greer of Dalfanso Engineering gave an overview of changes made since the last meeting. He stated
that the test pits came back, and the soil is excellent for building. Mr. Greer also stated that Mark
Hampton completed test pits, remapped the wetlands and provided a wetlands report. Also,
Stormwater Management has been redone.

Mr. Greer continued to say that buffering has changed slightly on the Cross Street side and that there is
a small row of maple trees that will be saved for natural buffers, with pine trees behind it for density. He
also noted that the invasive knotweed has been removed from the site.

Abutters were given an opportunity to ask questions and make comments regarding the proposal.
Nicole Toomey of 2 Plummers Landing asked about the natural boundaries, wetland impacts on her
property, and traffic flow.

Jennifer Preece of Sweden, but soon to be 494 Main St, Bridgton, asked about delivery scheduling and
impact on her property during construction.

Libby Graffam seconded Ms. Priests concerns regarding delivery scheduling and stated that the
residents in the area should be considered.

Brandon Toomey of 2 Plummers Landing commented that the large pine tree is one of the largest in the
State of Maine. Mr. Toomey also commented on wetland concerns, uses in town districts, and limiting
the use of the food pantry to Bridgton residents only.

Penny Robbins, 260 Pond Road, stated that Good Shepherd does not dictate hours of operation.

Chair Brusini and Rolf Madsen asked that a copy of the contract from the Good Shepherd be supplied to
the Board to ensure it does not exceed any of the standards.

Debbie Ogle of Kezar Heights voiced concerned regarding people from different communities being fed
partially by a block grant that is meant for Bridgton residents.

Tracy Martin, executive director for the Lakes Region Recovery Center in Bridgton, stated that it is our
job, as a community, to make sure that people don’t go hungry. She also stated that the unhoused are
already within the community, living in cars, unable to pay rent and/or childcare and the food pantry
ensure that they do not go hungry.

Libby Graffam stated to Ms. Martin that it needs to be considered that this is a residential area and if it
was constructed in her neighborhood that she might feel differently.

Dave Akins of Lower Main Street stated that he thinks the scale of the project is too large for a town the
size of Bridgton. He stated that he agrees that there is a need, but that this is not the right fit for
location.

Maureen Johnson of Pond Road stated that she has been a volunteer at the Harrison Food Pantry and
that in comparison, Bridgton Food Pantry is exceptional. She stated that these volunteers are here to
help and not many people do that.

Sarah Reed of 121 Wildwood Road stated that there are a lot of stigmas in the room around those who
utilize the food pantry, but they are mothers and children in need and as a community, we should help
one another.

Amy Oulette, volunteer for the Bridgton Food Pantry, stated that this is a food pantry, not a homeless
shelter. The volunteers will not be soliciting for people to stay in the pantry or housing them.

Leslie McAllister of 58 Sunnybrook Farm Rd stated that the Planning Board is going to base a decision on
facts and ordinances. Ms. McAllister also stated that the Bridgton Food Pantry does an excellent job of
catering to specific needs of its clients with health restrictions.
Norman Oulette, volunteer for the Bridgton Food Pantry, stated that he and his wife have volunteered
for 2 years at the pantry. He stated that there is fun and laughter in a time where most are discouraged.

Debbie Sullivan of North Road and volunteer at the Bridgton Food Pantry stated that she volunteers for
about 20 hours per week to the food pantry. She emphasized the importance of the food pantry to the
community, specifically to mothers and children.

Chris Green of Naples and volunteers at the Bridgton Food Pantry stated that the traffic is limited and
this is essential to the community.

Tom Greer stated that the volunteers have and continue to put in an extreme amount of time and effort
into running the pantry and supporting it and that the Planning Board should have enough information
at this time to decide.

Ella Nevill of 4 Oak Street stated that she supports the food pantry’s mission, but not the location and
impact on the neighborhood. Ms. Nevill also brought up concerns about signage, consideration for the
neighborhood, and zoning for businesses versus residential.

Reed Holden answered questions and addressed concerns from the public. He stated that they searched
for a location to rent and were not able to secure a space. Mr. Holden also stated that there is a
dumpster pad on the site plan, but there will not be a dumpster there, but it may be used for a bottle
depository in the future, but all food waste is taken off site and used for compost.

The Board began reviewing and had an opportunity to ask clarifying questions to the applicant.

Chair Brusini stated that the proposed project is classified as Institutional Use in the Downtown Village
Neighborhood, which was also the determination of the Code Enforcement Officer.

Rolf Madsen asked how the semi delivery trucks will deliver on a posted road in the Winter. Mr. Greer
stated that they will utilize the Lower Main Street entrance as that road is not posted. Discussion ensued
regarding David Madsen, Public Works Directors Impact Statement. It was determined that further
clarification is needed by Mr. Madsen on whether Lower Main Street is a posted road.

Chair Brusini asked Mr. Greer to explain how this proposal is consistent with the towns Comprehensive
Plan. She asked for a written narrative to be submitted.

Discussion ensued between the Board and Mr. Greer with questions regarding tree removal, surface
water drainage, lighting, parking, and noise from refrigeration units.

MOTION         Rolf Madsen moved to recess Public Hearing to be continued on August 19, 2025, at
5PM with information supplied to staff by August 12, 2025, second by Angie Cook
MOTION CARRIES 5/0
